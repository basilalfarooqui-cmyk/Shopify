What it tracks

Six signals, read continuously, all night.

The finger gives a stronger pulse reading than the wrist, so Orbit measures more with less power. Everything below is captured while you sleep and summarised into one number by morning.

Sleep stages

Deep, REM and light sleep separated minute by minute, with the interruptions you never remember in the morning.

Heart-rate variability

The clearest early signal of stress and recovery. Orbit reads it overnight, when nothing else is interfering.

Body temperature

Tenths of a degree, tracked against your own baseline — often the first hint of illness or a cycle shift.

Resting heart rate

Your lowest overnight rate and the hour it landed — the single most reliable marker of aerobic fitness.

Readiness score

One number each morning that weighs sleep, recovery and strain, so you know whether to push or to rest.

Daily movement

Steps, active minutes and calories, kept quietly in the background. No rings to close, no badges to chase.

Why a ring

A watch buzzes. A ring just listens.

Wrist wearables are compromised by design: a screen that demands attention, a battery that lasts a day, and a sensor sitting on the part of your body that moves most. Orbit removes all three problems by moving to the finger and dropping the screen entirely.

You charge it on a Sunday, put it on, and read one summary the next morning. That is the entire interaction.
